# Don K. Mak — Deep Research Brief

## Executive Summary

**Don K. Mak** is a Canadian-based physicist and former government research scientist who applies **signal processing mathematics** (Fourier analysis, phase analysis, frequency domain methods) to financial market trading. He is the author of 3 trading books published by World Scientific (2003, 2006) and Springer (2021), plus a general science methodology book translated into Korean, Chinese, and Arabic [1][2][3].

His approach is strictly mathematical/scientific — analyzing indicators from a DSP perspective, introducing concepts like "Profit Zone vs. Loss Zone" in the frequency spectrum, "skipped convolution" for reduced lag, and phase analysis to explain why certain indicators outperform others. He has published 50+ papers in condensed matter physics and was an Adjunct Professor at Queen's University, Canada [4]. He has **no connection** to TASC magazine, Gann analysis, or esoteric market methods.

## Theoretical Framework

### Core Approach: Phase/Frequency Analysis of Indicators

Mak's unique contribution is analyzing trading indicators through the lens of **signal processing** [1][2][3]:

1. **Fourier analysis of market data** — decomposing price into frequency components to understand what signals indicators actually extract
2. **Phase analysis** — explaining why some indicators lead or lag price turns based on their phase response in the frequency domain
3. **Profit Zone vs. Loss Zone** — dividing the frequency spectrum into regions where trading tactics are profitable vs. unprofitable
4. **Skipped convolution** — a technique to detect market movements earlier by reducing the inherent lag of moving-average-based indicators
5. **Indicators as calculus derivatives** — noting that oscillator indicators approximate first and second derivatives of price (velocity and acceleration)

### Key Insights

- The **Awesome Oscillator** (AO) approximates the first derivative (velocity) of price; the **Accelerator Oscillator** approximates the second derivative (acceleration) [3]
- **MACD/MACDH** can be tuned via parameters to change phase/time delay and potentially improve profitability [3]
- Different parameter choices move indicators between "Profit Zone" and "Loss Zone" in frequency space [3]
- Market turning points can be forecast by analyzing phase relationships [3]

### Comparison to Related Work

| Researcher | Approach | Relationship to Mak |
|-----------|----------|---------------------|
| **John Ehlers** | DSP filters (SuperSmoother, Laguerre, bandpass) | Same DSP tradition; Ehlers focuses on filter *design*, Mak on *analysis* of existing indicators |
| **William Blau** | Double smoothing (empirical 2nd-order IIR) | Mak provides the frequency-domain explanation for why Blau's approach works |
| **Tushar Chande** | Adaptive indicators (VMA, CMO) | Different angle on the same lag problem |
| **Perry Kaufman** | Adaptive moving averages (KAMA) | Practical; less theoretical than Mak |

Mak occupies a unique niche: he doesn't *invent* new indicators (like Ehlers or Blau), but provides the **signal processing theory** explaining why certain indicators and tactics work better than others.
