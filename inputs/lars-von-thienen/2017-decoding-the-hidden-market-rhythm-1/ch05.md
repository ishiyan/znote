# Decoding the Hidden Market Rhythm

## 5. CYCLES ARE NOT STATIC: THE DYNAMIC NATURE OF CYCLES

The assumption that cycles are static over time is misleading for trading purposes. Dominant cycles transform over time due to the nature of their internal length and phase parameters. Active dominant cycles do not abruptly jump from one length (e.g., 50) to another (e.g., 120). Typically, a dominant cycle remains active for an extended period and varies around its core parameters. The "genes" of the cycle—length, phase, and amplitude—are not fixed and will transform around the dominant parameters.

Such periodic movements abound in nature and the human-made world. Examples include a heartbeat or the cyclical movements of planets. While many real movements inherently repeat, few are perfectly periodic. For instance, a walker’s stride frequency may vary, and a heart may beat slower or faster. Once a person is in a dominant state (such as sitting and writing a book), their heartbeat stabilizes to approximately 85 beats per minute. However, the exact cycle will not remain static at 85 BPM but may vary by +/- 10%. This variance is not considered a new heartbeat cycle at 87 BPM or 83 BPM; it is still regarded as the same dominant active vibration.

This pattern can also be observed in environmental systems and mathematical equations. Real cyclical movements are not perfectly uniform; the period varies slightly from one cycle to the next due to changes in physical environmental factors.

This dynamic behavior also applies to financial market cycles. Anticipating current values for cycle length and phase in real time is crucial for identifying the next turn. Understanding the active parameters of the dominant cycle and verifying and tracking the real-time situation—including dynamic variations—enables projection of the next significant event.

### 5.1 Effect A: Changes in Cycle Length

The first effect is the contraction and expansion of cycles, or the "cycle breath." Possible cycles are detected from available data on the left side of the chart. Points A and B show an acceptable fit between both cycles. However, the dynamic red cycle has a longer length parameter. Past data reveal this is not critical, and there is a good fit between the theoretical static and dynamic cycles at points A and B. Unfortunately, in the future projection area on the right side of the chart—where trading occurs—a growing deviation emerges between the static and dynamic cycles. The difference between the static and dynamic cycles at points C and D is now relatively high.

![Figure 5-1](./assets/fig-5-1.png)

**Figure 5-1:** Effect 1 - Cycle with extraction and contraction

The "dynamic" real cycle has a parameter with a slightly greater length. The consequence is that future deviations increase even when discrepancies between the theoretical and real cycles are not visible in the analysis area. These differences are crucial for trading. As trading occurs on the right side of the chart, the current parameters for the next expected cycle turn must be detected.

A perfect fit with past data or a two-year projection is not the priority. The focus is the here and now, not a mathematical fit with the past. Current market turns must align with the dynamic cycle to detect the next turn. Like an individual’s heartbeat cycle approaching a base number, cycle length varies around the dominant parameter by +/- 5%. Relying solely on the theoretical static cycle will not provide information about expected turning points. However, this is not the only effect.

### 5.2 Effect B: Changes in Cycle Phase

The next effect is "offset shifts." In this case, the cycle length parameter is the same between the theoretical static and dynamic cycles. The dynamic cycle at point A shows a slight shift in the offset at the peak. Mathematically, the phase parameter has transformed. This effect remains fixed in the future. A static deviation is observed between highs and lows.

![Figure 5-2](./assets/fig-5-2.png)

**Figure 5-2:** Effect 2 - Cycle with phase distortion

Though not a one-time effect, the dominant cycle’s phase will also continuously change by +/- 5% around the central dominant parameters.

### 5.3 Combined Effects

In practice, both effects occur in parallel and change continuously around the central dominant parameters. Figure 3 presents a snapshot of these two effects with the theoretical and dynamic cycles. The deviation in the projection area at points C and D shows that relying solely on the theoretical static cycle quickly becomes invalid.

![Figure 5-3](./assets/fig-5-3.png)

**Figure 5-3:** Combined effects. The combined effects (Gray: Static Cycle / Red: Dynamic Cycle) show that the deviation is significant—at point D, a cycle high is expected for the theoretical static cycle (gray), while the real dynamic cycle (red) remains low at point D.

These two effects occur continuously. Although alignment in the past (points A and B) appears acceptable between the static and dynamic cycles, the deviation in the projection area (points C and D) is so high that trading the static cycle will lead to failure.

An example of cycle forecasting incorporating these effects explains the consequences on the right side of the chart.

While a dominant cycle can be detected in the left area of the chart, the detailed dynamic parameters are the significant differentiators and are crucial for a valid and credible projection. Classic static cycle projections often fail for this reason. Detecting the active dominant cycle represents only one part of the process.

The second part involves considering the current dynamic parameters regarding length and phase. Although a perfect fit of a cycle in the distant past between price and a static cycle may seem compelling mathematically, it is misleading because it ignores dynamic cycle components. While this simplifies calculations, it holds no value for trading on the right side of the chart. Examining static cycles for a perfect fit in the past is unnecessary. Observing two to five significant correlations of tops and bottoms while considering updates to current dynamic components will yield valid trading cycle projections.

This example underscores the importance of an approach combining a dominant cycle detection engine with updates to dynamic components.
