## 7. Dynamic Cycles in Gold and Silver - From Theory to Reality

It's time to review the Cycle Explorer in action: The following chapters examine some live examples conducted publicly using the Cycle Explorer on gold and silver. Monday, April 15, 2013, marked the largest one-day drop in gold/silver markets, prompting the following headline in financial media.

![Figure 7-1](./assets/fig-7-1.png)

**Figure 7-1:** Bloomberg Headline News, April 15, 2013 [2]

Gold and silver futures experienced their largest single-day decline since the 1980s on April 15, 2013, with silver dropping 11% in just one day.

A brief review of silver markets from a cycle analyst's perspective shows that this event was expected and not surprising at all.

In 2011, silver futures underwent a parabolic upward movement. This scenario was monitored in the public domain, demonstrating the application of cycle tools in real-time trading.

### 7.1 Dynamic Cycles Projection: April/May 2011

![Figure 7-2](./assets/fig-7-2.png)

**Figure 7-2:** Parabolic upward movement in silver futures - April 27, 2011

The critical parabolic juncture in April 2011 was anticipated, and a cycle analysis was issued beforehand to highlight this significant event. [3]

The analysis revealed a dominant cycle with a length of 117 trading days, which had been solid and stable since 2009! Therefore, there was a high probability that this 117-day cycle could forecast the next major turning points. The dominant cycle was already active and detected in 2009 (see Figure 7-3) - a bar-by-bar tutorial from 2009 to the day of the analysis (May 2011) showed that this 117-day cycle projected all turning points from 2009 to 2011.

![Figure 7-3](./assets/fig-7-3.png)

**Figure 7-3:** Detected 116-day cycle at the end of 2009 (Published in Public Forum) [4]

The projected highs and lows are represented by the words HIGH/LOW in the cycle plotted for 2010/2011.

During 2010, the cycle remained active. The dynamic cycle explorer tracked shifts in offset (the cycle low causing a +/- movement in some bars) and cycle breathing (lengths changing between 115 and 118 days). Figure 7-4 shows the cycle updated through May 2011. The anchoring and adjustment of the dynamic cycle explorer indicator remained unchanged. The script log detecting the 117-day cycle length showed the cycle stayed active. The blue dashed line represents the current cycle projection, based on dynamic changes to offset and breathing.

The labels marked “HIGH”/“LOW” in Figure 7-4 have not been changed. These labels were placed based on the analysis and forward projection from the end of 2009 (see Figure 7-3). Dynamic shifting is visible in this cycle due to a slight deviation between the blue dashed line indicated by the new projection and the old text markers (see Figure 7-4).

![Figure 7-4](./assets/fig-7-4.png)

**Figure 7-4:** Real-time public warning regarding a major top based on a 117-day cycle (May 3, 2011); high/low labels were written for plotting from late 2009 [5]

Here, the dynamic behavior is observed. Even with a consistent dominant cycle, a static projection should not be followed. The dynamic component must be updated after each new bar to align the “arrival” date of the next projected low or high based on current changes.

This same cycle, updated by the dynamic component, now projected an expected high in silver futures on May 3, 2011. This analysis was conducted live and published in a public internet forum.

I warned about silver's parabolic movement during the preceding weeks. While it is difficult to anticipate the end of a parabolic move, cycles can guide analysis by detecting and projecting the current dominant cycle. The dynamic cycle explorer is the appropriate tool.

The dynamic cycle explorer indicated the end of the parabolic movement within days after the price began to fall. If a parabolic move ends and a dominant cycle projects a top, this presents an interesting trading opportunity. We noted on May 3, 2011, that this cycle projected all turning points in 2010. This led me to publicly share this analysis on May 3, 2011, after silver's price surged above $45 and the dominant cycle projected a major top.

Over the next four days after the projection and public cycle chart, silver futures fell 22% to $35.

![Figure 7-5](./assets/fig-7-5.png)

**Figure 7-5:** Price update - Silver futures dropped 22% after the call

This represents a perfect example of detecting and predicting cyclic market events. I recall this example because it remained stable between 2009 and projected the major 2011 market top.

### 7.3 Dynamic Cycles Projection: 2012 and March 2013

Returning to the Bloomberg news headline introduced at the beginning of this chapter, silver prices dropped by over 10% during one day in April 2013. The headlines reflected the surprise of both the public and industry professionals. However, the reaction would have been very different had the dominant silver cycle outlined received due recognition.

The identified and tracked cycle remained active in 2013. This implies that the dominant cycle, with a length of 117 trading days, projected all turning points between 2011 and 2013. The cycle revealed all major tops and bottoms for two more years after detection by the Dynamic Cycle Explorer and public sharing in 2011.

Figure 7-9 illustrates a static cycle account to show that the distance between tops and bottoms is approximately 117 bars The cycle explorer guides you precisely through these points, as shown in the examples.

![Figure 7-9](./assets/fig-7-9.png)

**Figure 7-9:** Static cycle review of the 117-day dominant cycle mentioned since May 2011

The drop in April 2013 was in the precise section of the expected cyclic slowdown projected to begin in February 2013. The market delivered. Market events regarding silver provided a textbook example of successful detection, projection, and trading of dominant cycles. This cycle was stable to the extent that even the static plot - as shown in Figure 7-9 - reflects an accurate cycle.

If you had followed this dominant cycle through the dynamic cycle explorer, the exact daily shifts of projected tops and bottoms could be tracked in real-time and updated bar-by-bar. Let’s begin a detailed guide starting from February 2013. A short-term dominant cycle with a length of 40 days was active and shown for the gold market. This short-term dynamic cycle was tracked between December 2012 and revealed in the open Wave59 forum on February 22, 2013, by a cycles toolkit client.

A preview of the expected low on February 22 and the expected high at the end of March was published.
The chart in Figure 7-10 attached to the post showed the detected cycle projecting a low on February 23 and the next high expected at the end of March.
