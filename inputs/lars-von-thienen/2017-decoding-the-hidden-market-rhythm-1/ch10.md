### 10.1 The Swing Cycle Method: Cycle Acceleration

We measure the cycle’s state by tracking the dominant cyclic component’s momentum. To compute this momentum, we project the cycle one bar ahead, then derive the momentum of the detrended cyclic component using:

> Cycle swing = acceleration of the dominant cycle in the current bar.

Cycle swing data is calculated for every bar. Since we track phase, amplitude, and wavelength shift in real time, we get dynamic, updated insights into the cycle’s underlying forces — **cycle acceleration**.

Note: This data comes from detrended price action. The indicator reflects the cyclic force pulling the real trend. The calculation centers on the dominant cycle’s acceleration, recalculated with each new bar. The output plots like any other indicator.

#### Evaluation Criteria for the New Swing Cycle Indicator

To assess this indicator’s trading potential, let’s define key measurements and compare it against one of today’s best-in-class indicators: Ultra Smooth Momentum (USM). Since the swing cycle concept resembles price momentum, USM serves as a useful benchmark. The main criteria for technical indicators are:

- Smoothness
- Zero lag
- Sharpness
- Robustness and adaptability to market conditions
- Accurate divergence detection

We’ll explore each criterion in detail.

- Smoothness
  There are two common ways to create a smooth indicator curve:
  1. Adjusting the indicator’s calculation length;
  2. Adding a smoothing algorithm—simple moving average, exponential moving average, or weighted moving average—on top of the indicator.
  However, moving averages rely on past data, introducing lag. Trading demands timely signals, and any delay destroys profitable entry and exit points. So, we face a contradiction:
  - More smoothing → more lag → late entries after most of the move has already happened.
  - Less smoothing → less lag → whipsaw trades.
  The solution? Proprietary smoothing algorithms that deliver both smoothness and zero lag — but often at the cost of sharpness.
- Sharpness
  When smoothing an indicator, a frequent downside is lost sharpness at turning points. Even with zero lag, the curve’s excessive smoothness can obscure key reversals. So, our third criterion is sharpness—**smooth, lag-free, and sharp**. Sharp peaks on the smooth indicator allow real-time signal visibility under actual trading conditions. In live trading, the "correct side" of the indicator isn’t visible—only a sharp, lag-free turn matters.
- Robustness and Adaptability to Market Conditions
 This criterion was addressed in earlier chapters on tuning standard indicators. Most indicators rely on adjusting calculation length, which must align with market vibration. Incorrect length settings significantly degrade signal quality. I’ve shown how to use the cycle engine to select optimal settings before the trading day starts. But an ideal indicator would automatically adapt to market vibration—robust, length-independent, and capable of self-adjusting its parameters to match the current dominant cycle.
- Accurate Divergence Detection
  High-probability signals often appear as divergences between the oscillator and price (momentum, RSI). An indicator that reliably detects market extremes with clear divergence plots ranks higher for trading use.

Several advanced indicators exist with optimized algorithms for smoothness and zero lag, such as:

- Adaptive Moving Average (AMA) and Ultra Smooth Momentum (USM) in Wave59;
- Jurik Research’s indicators (VEL, JMA).

### 10.2 Adaptive Divergence Detection: Repeating Patterns in Cycle Peaks and Troughs

The Swing Cycle Indicator’s main purpose is detecting high-probability setups at cycle peaks and troughs. These cycle tops share a common pattern:

When a cycle peaks, the price stalls. The emotions driving extreme price action drain the cycle’s pure energy. You see the price form a higher high (or lower low) as market participants overshoot emotionally—even though the driving energy is already fading, forming a lower high (or higher low). This behavior is universal at cycle extremes.

The Swing Cycle Indicator excels at measuring these situations. At every major turn, you’ll see this pattern: a divergence between price and swing cycle. The explanation is simple:

When cycle energy slows (measured by the swing cycle) while market emotions remain extreme (price action), an imbalance forms between cyclic forces and price movement — one that resolves quickly. These are the high-probability setups we’re after.

### 10.3 Trading Swing Cycle Indicator Turns: Staying in Market Vibration

There are additional ways to use the Swing Cycle Indicator for trading—any oscillator-based technique works. Here’s another critical method:

The swing cycle is fast, sharp, and smooth, making its turns ideal for trading. The idea resembles classic oscillator techniques like RSI or Stochastic, where overbought/oversold zones signal trades. Instead of fixed bands, the swing cycle uses adaptive bands tracking recent behavior.

Trading Rules:

- SELL: When the Swing Cycle Indicator breaks below the upper adaptive band. The cycle has likely peaked, and the next move is downward.
- BUY: When the Swing Cycle Indicator breaks above the lower adaptive band. The cycle has likely bottomed, and the next move is upward.
