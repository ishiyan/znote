# Decoding the Hidden Market Rhythm

## 11. GROUP CYCLE: COMBINING VOLUME, RANGE, AND PRICE CYCLES

The advantages of applying multi-timeframe analysis when using technical indicators are well documented. When signals from longer and shorter timeframes align, the effectiveness and probability of technical signals increase significantly. By adding cycle-based analysis to our trading arsenal, we can introduce another multi-chart framework technique, which closely resembles the multi-timeframe approach.

Cycles don’t just manifest in time. Several cycles play out during a trading day. Our time-based charts only provide a time-centric view of these cycles. By applying cycle analysis tools to price charts, we aim to detect dominant time-based cycles and identify expected trend changes based on timing.

The trend changes (TCIs) visible in price charts during an intraday session aren’t solely attributable to time-based cycles. Volume is another critical factor—without it, most TCIs can’t be detected using cycle tools on time-based charts.

A new trading technique emerges when we combine time-based cycle analysis with other key market cycles.

### 11.1 Volume Cycles

One of the most important forms of cyclical behavior, especially in intraday futures charts, is volume. On any given day, there’s a fixed amount of capital—or "traders"—looking to enter or exit the market. For example, if all market participants are fully invested on the long side based on available volume, the market will decline regardless of time-based technical signals. No volume remains to push the current condition higher. Volume tells us a decline is coming before the market can rise again. Such signals only become visible when we detect, monitor, and project the dominant volume cycle currently available for trading.

To apply this form of volume cycle analysis, we first need a volume-based chart instead of a time-based one. Charting tools that support this allow plotting bars where each represents a fixed volume amount. A new bar forms once traded volume exceeds the defined threshold. Second, we need a charting tool that detects, tracks, and forecasts dominant cycles representing the underlying strength of price movements. A projected dominant cycle on a volume chart provides insight into future inflection points based on upcoming volume bars—not time. These projections refer to a quantity of traded volume where the next inflection might occur, even if the bars visually resemble time-based charts. Volume-based and time-based charts measure entirely independent factors: time and volume. Volume charts are ideal for applying cycle tools because volume cycles play a significant role, particularly in intraday charts.

Let’s examine how to combine signals from time-based charts and volume-based charts. We’ll use a single cycle tool: the Swing Cycle Indicator (SCI). This indicator measures momentum based on the currently active dominant cycle rather than relying on lagging price data. It detects active cycles and uses the acceleration of the underlying dominant cycle to signal momentum. The result is an ultra-sharp, leading, and smooth momentum indicator based on cycle analysis.

The following chart illustrates this cycle analysis technique on a volume-based Emini futures chart over a full week (February 11–15). Each bar represents 15k volume. Trading signals, marked on the chart, derive from divergences and extreme turns in the swing cycle indicator. This indicator is particularly effective for divergences because it’s leading, precise at turns, and smooth during noisy periods—a feature unavailable in standard indicators, where divergence trading is often impossible. Using the SCI, divergences typically appear 1–2 bars before the turn unfolds.

Divergences indicate when volume flow must shift to resolve current extremes—under such conditions, price simply follows volume. Traders must switch from long to short positions to free up sufficient volume for a new upward move. Without available volume for fresh long positions, price must decline first—and vice versa. This is a critical cycle to track on volume-based charts.

### 11.2 Price Range Cycles

Another key cycle is measured purely by price. Beyond volume and time-based cycles, every instrument follows its own intrinsic price pattern. A contract moves from one price range to another in cycles. These are "vertical" cycles, plotted on the price axis—not the "horizontal" axis of volume or time. To detect these cycles, we switch to price-range charts, where a new bar forms after a specified price range is exceeded. This means a contract moves in fixed price steps—up and down. Now, introducing the cycle toolset allows us to detect the dominant underlying price range of our trading vehicle. By applying cycle tools, we measure yet another independent cycle: price-range cycles, unconnected to volume or time. This is what makes cycle analysis unique!

Now, the full potential of multi-cycle trading unfolds: almost every major trend change was detected by our cycle indicator. Mapping signals to the time-based chart is essential because we live and trade in the time domain. Thus, all three chart types (volume, price, and time) must be monitored simultaneously in a real trading environment alongside cycle indicators. Signals are then mapped onto the time-based chart, which serves as the primary execution chart. This approach reveals that all turns are primarily driven by cyclical behavior—pure natural law observable in price charts. However, time-based cycles aren’t the sole factor influencing market behavior. This explains why many critical inflection points are hidden in time-based charts. It accounts for the triple divergences in time-based charts: they arise because volume and/or price cycles aren’t yet aligned with time cycles—they still need to play out. When volume and price cycles synchronize with time, however, it’s time to act. In other words, don’t focus solely on time-based cycles. By adding volume and price-range cycles to time-based cycles, you can decode nearly all market movements in intraday charts. You’ll see and decode such critical trading signals if you apply the potential of cycles to volume and range charts.

It’s critical to analyze cycles across these three chart types—not just purely technical indicators. Cycles are the driving force, not price plots.

To summarize: the highest potential emerges when time, volume, and price-range cycles align to form a cycle cluster. These events are highlighted in yellow on the time-based chart, numbered 1 to 4. After each alignment, the week’s most significant moves began to unfold. This is what to look for in real-time trading environments. The chart shows a full week of uninterrupted intraday trading action, revealing four clusters. You’ll be rewarded if you pay close attention to all three cycle frames and wait for alignment when cycles come into play. This is when you want to place your intraday trades.
