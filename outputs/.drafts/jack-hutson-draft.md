# Jack K. Hutson: The Man Behind TRIX and TASC Magazine

## Executive Summary

Jack K. Hutson is a Boeing mechanical engineer turned financial publisher who founded *Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities* (TASC) magazine in 1982 and invented the TRIX (Triple Exponential Average) indicator. Despite creating one of the most widely implemented technical indicators in trading software worldwide, Hutson remains a remarkably private figure with no confirmed public photographs, video interviews, or conference recordings available online.

---

## 1. Biography

### Background and Early Career

Jack K. Hutson worked as a mechanical engineer at Boeing in Seattle, Washington. His middle name is unknown beyond the initial "K." No birth date, educational institution, or other early biographical details have been confirmed in public sources.

Hutson had a brief foray into the stock market in the late 1960s, then moved into real estate in the 1970s (purchasing two additional houses). He returned to securities trading around 1980, purchasing a personal computer system for approximately $7,500 to run charting software [1].

### The Path to TASC

At Boeing, Hutson collaborated with colleague Anthony Warren, who held a PhD in mathematics. Together they worked on improving technical analysis software. Around 1981–82, Hutson was invited to a technicians' conference in Toronto where he presented a paper that became the foundation of TASC's inaugural issue [1][2].

## 2. TASC Magazine

Hutson founded *Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities* in October 1982. His byline in the first issue reads "JACK K. HUTSON, EDITOR" [3]. The publishing entity is Technical Analysis, Inc., based in Seattle, WA.

Key facts:
- **First issue:** October 1982
- **Initial subscription price:** $250/year
- **Early growth:** 1,500 subscribers by 1983; 10,000+ by 1984
- **2013 subscriber count:** 60,000 [4]
- **Cumulative reach:** 1,226,237 subscribers from 174 countries since 1982 [3]
- **Current website:** https://traders.com

The magazine was the first dedicated periodical for technical analysis of financial markets and remains in continuous publication. By 1988 it operated from the converted Fauntleroy Elementary School in West Seattle with 12 employees [1].

## 3. The TRIX Indicator

### Origin

Hutson introduced TRIX in two TASC articles:
- "Good Trix" — July 1983 [3]
- "TRIX: Triple Exponential Smoothing Oscillator" — May 1984 [3]

### Formula

```
1. EMA1 = EMA(Close, n)              — Single exponential smoothing
2. EMA2 = EMA(EMA1, n)              — Double exponential smoothing  
3. EMA3 = EMA(EMA2, n)              — Triple exponential smoothing
4. TRIX = ((EMA3_today - EMA3_yesterday) / EMA3_yesterday) × 100
```

**Default parameters:** n = 15 periods; signal line = 9-period EMA of TRIX [5].

### Design Intent

TRIX was designed to:
- **Filter noise** — triple smoothing eliminates minor price fluctuations
- **Identify trends** — positive TRIX = rising triple EMA (bullish); negative = bearish
- **Generate signals** — via signal line crossovers, centerline crossovers, and divergences

### Comparison to MACD

| Feature | TRIX (15,9) | MACD (12,26,9) |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Smoothness | Higher (fewer whipsaws) | More responsive |
| Lag | Greater | Lower |
| Construction | Rate of change of triple EMA | Difference of two EMAs |
| Zero line | Yes | Yes |
| Signal line | 9-period EMA | 9-period EMA |

TRIX produces fewer false signals than MACD at the cost of greater lag [5].

### Other Indicators

TRIX is the only indicator reliably attributed to Hutson as inventor. As TASC editor, he was instrumental in popularizing many other concepts (Wyckoff Method, Fourier analysis for markets, Elliott Wave comparisons) but did not claim invention of additional indicators.
