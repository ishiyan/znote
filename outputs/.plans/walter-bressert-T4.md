## Goal
Provide a critical, evidence-based assessment of Bressert's methodology: is cycle-based market timing and the Double Smoothed Stochastic supported by independent/academic evidence, or is it primarily practitioner folklore and marketing? Be even-handed.

## Questions
1. **Academic literature on market cycles & timing**: Search arXiv, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, SSRN for evidence on:
   - Predictability of fixed/periodic cycles in futures/stock prices (the efficient-market critique; spectral analysis findings; the general failure of fixed-period cycle forecasting).
   - J.M. Hurst cyclic model validity — any academic treatment.
   - Stochastic oscillator / double-smoothed oscillator performance studies.
   Report what the literature actually says (likely: little support for fixed cycles; oscillators are lagging/curve-fit prone). Cite specific papers where found.
2. **The detrending/centered-MA method**: Note the well-known statistical caveat — a centered moving average uses future data, so cycle "tops/bottoms" it reveals are only known with a lag of half the cycle length (hindsight bias). This is a legitimate methodological critique. Document it.
3. **Timing bands & the "middle 70%"**: Assess as a forecasting method — is keeping the central 70% of a historical interval distribution statistically meaningful, or is it descriptive? Note non-stationarity of market cycles.
4. **Marketing vs. substance**: Bressert sold software (CycleTrader/ProfitTrader ~$195–595), courses, and newsletters. Note that much surviving material is on course-reseller / vendor sites. Flag the conflict-of-interest / survivorship issues without ad hominem.
5. **Counterpoint (steelman)**: What's genuinely useful in his framework regardless of cycle validity? (Disciplined entry rules, defined-risk money management, multi-contract scaling, combining momentum with timing) — these are risk-management practices that stand independent of cycle-forecasting claims.
