# Gerald Appel — Research Brief

> **Subject:** Gerald Appel (1933–2020), creator of the MACD indicator, investment advisor, and technical analysis author.

## Biography

**Gerald Appel** (March 25, 1933 – December 25, 2020) was an American investor, technical analyst, and author who created the Moving Average Convergence/Divergence (MACD) indicator in the late 1970s. He founded **Signalert Corporation**, a registered investment advisory firm based in Great Neck, New York, which he ran for over 40 years managing assets for individual and institutional clients. He also published the investment newsletter **Systems & Forecasts** for several decades.

Appel held a degree in psychoanalysis and practiced as a psychoanalyst before transitioning to full-time money management. He was a prolific author, writing approximately 15 books on investing and technical analysis, and contributed numerous articles to *Technical Analysis of Stocks & Commodities* (TASC) magazine and other financial publications.

He passed away on December 25, 2020, at the age of 87.

### Notes on Attribution

- **PPO** is the percentage-normalized form of MACD. StockCharts describes it as "a percentage version of MACD" using the same (12, 26, 9) parameters. It is implicitly Appel's invention since it's a direct mathematical transformation of MACD, though the name "PPO" was coined later by charting software vendors.
- **MACD Histogram**: Appel used "histogram" to refer to bar plots of the MACD line itself. Thomas Aspray (1986) introduced the now-standard definition (MACD minus Signal Line) as a separate contribution.
- The **19/39-day EMA crossover** is one of Appel's most famous timing systems, published in *Systems & Forecasts* and widely cited.
- The **Advance-Decline timing models** in *Power Tools* (2005) combine NYSE breadth with momentum filters — these are Appel's original composite systems.

## Notes on Research Limitations

1. **TASC Archive**: A comprehensive scan of all TASC TOC XMLs (1986–2015, every month + bonus issues) confirmed only 1 article authored by Appel. The Mar 1994 interview was conducted by Thom Hartle. Appel published primarily through his own channels (books, *Systems & Forecasts* newsletter).
2. **Wikipedia**: There is no Wikipedia page for Gerald Appel the technical analyst (the "Gerald Appel" Wikipedia page is about Gerald B. Appel, a nephrologist — a different person entirely).
3. **Obituaries**: Appel's death on December 25, 2020 is widely referenced in financial community sources but formal obituaries were not retrievable via the tools available.
4. **Birth year**: Some sources cite 1933; this is based on his reported age of 87 at death in 2020.
