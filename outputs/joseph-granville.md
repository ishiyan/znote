# Joseph Granville — Research Brief

## Biography

**Joseph Ensign Granville** (August 20, 1923 – September 7, 2013) was an American financial writer, market analyst, and investment seminar speaker who became one of the most famous—and controversial—stock market forecasters of the 20th century.

| Year | Event |
|------|-------|
| 1923 | Born August 20, United States (likely Yonkers, New York) |
| ~1941–1945 | Served in U.S. Navy during World War II |
| ~1947 | Graduated from Duke University (BA in Business Administration) |
| 1960 | Published *A Strategy of Daily Stock Market Timing for Maximum Profit* |
| 1963 | Published *Granville's New Key to Stock Market Profits* introducing On-Balance Volume |
| 1963–2013 | Published *The Granville Market Letter* newsletter (~50 years) |
| 1976 | Published *Granville's New Strategy of Daily Stock Market Timing for Maximum Profit* |
| Apr 22, 1980 | Market call attributed with moving DJIA +4.05% |
| Jan 6, 1981 | "Sell everything" call caused ~$25 billion market loss |
| 1982 | Newsletter had 20,000+ subscribers; revenues exceeded $6M/year |
| Sep 7, 2013 | Died age 90 in Kansas City, Missouri, of pneumonia |

### The 1981 Market Crash Call

On January 6, 1981, Granville issued a late-night "sell everything" recommendation to his subscribers. The next morning, the Dow Jones Industrial Average plunged, with major media sources reporting approximately $25 billion in market losses. According to Robert Shiller, this was one of the most dramatic instances of a single analyst moving the entire market. This event cemented Granville's reputation as the most influential market letter writer of his era.

### Theatrical Presentations

Granville was legendary for his showmanship at investment conferences:
- Emerged from a coffin on stage
- Appeared to walk across water (over a swimming pool) when meeting clients
- Performed magic tricks and wore costumes
- Arrived at seminars in outlandish outfits
- Conducted his presentations as entertainment spectacles rather than dry lectures

His flamboyance made him a fixture on television (CNBC, Wall Street Week with Louis Rukeyser) and a media celebrity throughout the 1970s–1990s.

### Legacy

Granville was known for persistent bearish calls during the 1970s, 1980s, and 1990s. While his 1981 call was spectacularly timed, his continued bearishness through the great bull market of the 1980s and 1990s ultimately cost subscribers. Edward Thorp analyzed his track record in the *Journal of Portfolio Management* (Spring 1982). Despite the controversy, his invention of On-Balance Volume remains one of the most widely used and universally implemented technical indicators in existence.

### Indicators Introduced in Books

#### Granville's New Key to Stock Market Profits (1963)

| Indicator | Category | Description |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| On-Balance Volume (OBV) | Volume | Cumulative running total of volume; adds volume on up-close days, subtracts on down-close days. Predicts price moves via volume divergence. |
| Net Field Trend Indicator | Trend | Measures the net direction of a basket of stocks using field theory concepts |
| OBV Trend Analysis | Volume | Pattern analysis applied to OBV line (double tops/bottoms, breakouts) |

#### Granville's New Strategy of Daily Stock Market Timing for Maximum Profit (1976)

| Indicator | Category | Description |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| Climax Indicator | Volume | Market breadth measure using volume climax conditions |
| OBV Trigger / Signal Line | Volume | Moving average or derivative applied to OBV as a signal generator |
| Daily volume analysis framework | Volume | Systematic methodology for volume-based market timing |
